Beagle Winter Coats and Booties: When Beagles Wear Them, How to Size a Deep-Chested Hound, and Gear That Actually Stays On

Who really needs winter gear, the three measurements that matter on a barrel-chested scenthound, and the design details that keep a coat and four boots on through a sniff-heavy walk.

Home›Blog›Winter Coats and Booties

Published: 2026-11-01 · Beagle Club Editors

Beagle winter coats and booties confuse a lot of owners, because the same hound that was bred to run all day in the field can stand on a cold sidewalk lifting one paw and staring at you. Whether your beagle needs gear depends less on the breed name and more on that particular dog, the weather and the walk. When the answer is yes, the hard part is the fit: a deep chest, a short back and a nose that is always on the ground knock most off-the-shelf coats out of place.

Quick answer

  • A healthy adult beagle with a typical dense coat often manages short winter walks without a coat. Puppies, seniors, thin-coated or very lean beagles, and dogs out in wet, windy weather are the ones most likely to benefit.
  • Size a beagle coat by chest girth first, then check back length and neck. Beagles carry a deep chest, so a coat chosen by back length alone is often tight behind the front legs.
  • Coats stay put when they have a wide belly strap or a wrap-style closure and a leash opening that lines up with the harness ring.
  • Booties stay on when they are sized to the paw width, fastened above the wrist or ankle, and introduced indoors with treats before the first snowy walk.

Do beagles need winter coats at all?

Not always. A fit adult beagle with a proper hound coat often handles a brisk winter walk without help, while puppies, older dogs, lean or thin-coated beagles and dogs out in wet, windy weather are the ones most likely to need one.

The breed was built for weather. The official beagle standard published by the National Beagle Club of America asks for "a close, hard, hound coat of medium length" and lists "a short, thin coat, or of a soft quality" as a defect. That tells you two things. First, a typical beagle coat is meant to shed rain and brush, so many beagles do fine on short outings. Second, not every pet beagle has that coat. Some have softer or thinner coats, and those dogs feel the cold sooner.

Which beagles benefit most

The UK veterinary charity PDSA, in its vet Q&A on winter walks, says dogs "that are very young, underweight, old or unwell need a warm dog coat when they go out," while dogs with thick fur who stay active usually do not. For beagle households that translates into a short list:

  • Puppies that are still filling out and spend a lot of time sitting during training.
  • Senior beagles that walk slower and stop more often.
  • Lean beagles. A beagle slimmed down to a healthy shape, which we discuss in our look at what the beagle obesity statistic really means, has less insulation than it used to. Re-measure after any big weight change, because the coat size changes too.
  • Thin or soft-coated beagles, including many mixes.
  • Any beagle on a slow, sniff-heavy walk in wet weather. A beagle tracking a scent at a crawl does not generate much heat, and a wet coat stops working as a barrier.

How your beagle tells you it is cold

Watch the dog, not the thermometer. Shivering, lifting paws one after another, a tucked tail, turning back toward home and refusing to sniff are all signs to shorten the walk and consider a layer next time. A beagle that ignores a fresh rabbit trail is not enjoying itself.

How do you measure a deep-chested beagle for a coat?

Measure three things with the dog standing: chest girth at the widest point just behind the front legs, neck where the collar sits, and back length from the collar to the base of the tail. For a beagle, choose the size by chest girth and use the other two numbers to rule out styles.

That order matters for this breed. The beagle standard calls for a chest that is "deep and broad", and a back that is "short, muscular and strong" with ribs "well sprung." Many coats are graded by back length, so a coat that looks right along the spine can pinch behind the elbows. Angela Cuce, co-founder of a company that makes clothing for broad-chested dogs, told Vetstreet that the chest "is the hardest area to fit" and that many jackets close under the neck and belly while leaving the chest exposed. She advised always having three measurements, neck, girth and length, and warned that if you size up for the neck or chest, "you may need to alter the length."

Taking the girth measurement

Ruffwear's sizing guide describes the same spot: "the widest part of your dog's chest just behind the front legs." Use a soft tailor's tape. No tape? Wrap a piece of string, mark where it meets, and lay it along a ruler. Measure twice. Beagles tend to suck in, lean or sit halfway through, so a helper with a treat held at nose height makes this a lot easier.

Taking length and neck

PDSA suggests measuring length from the neck, where the collar sits, to the base of the tail, plus chest width behind the front legs and neck size, and writing everything down in both centimeters and inches because coats are sold in both. Keep the numbers on your phone. You will need them again when you check a size chart in a store.

Reading a size chart for a beagle

Ruffwear's apparel and harness girth bands run XXS 13 to 17 in, XS 17 to 22 in, S 22 to 27 in, M 27 to 32 in and L 32 to 36 in. A beagle whose girth sits right on a boundary is the classic in-between dog. If your beagle already wears a harness, the girth you used there is the one that matters here too; our beagle harness size chart compares how the same girth lands in different brands.

What makes a beagle coat stay on?

An adjustable belly strap or wrap closure that sits behind the front legs, a chest panel that covers the underside, and a leash opening that lines up with the harness clip. Those three details matter more than the brand name or the filling.

Beagles walk with the nose down and the shoulders working, which constantly drags the front of a coat forward and sideways. A strap under the belly stops that rotation. Cuce told Vetstreet that a belly belt on her company's jackets gives "an additional 1 to 2 inches" of adjustment, which is exactly the slack a beagle between sizes needs.

Check the underside

Cuce also pointed out that "most dogs' coats are very sparse on the underbelly," the part closest to snow and wet ground. Beagles are low enough that the chest and belly pick up slush on every walk. A coat that covers only the back keeps the least important part warm.

Movement and toileting test

PDSA says your dog "should be able to move freely in their coat," including running, jumping and going to the toilet, and that biting at the coat, scratching or rubbing against furniture is a sign the fit is wrong. For male beagles, check that the belly panel finishes well ahead of where it would get wet.

What other hound owners report

Owners of basenjis, another short-coated hound built with a large rib cage, compare notes in a long-running winter coat thread on a breed forum. One owner found that a jacket that fastens with hook-and-loop on top worked best for the breed's "large rib cages and harness", and another said they only buy hook-and-loop coats because they are less hassle to put on and take off. That matches what we hear from beagle owners: wrap styles beat pull-overs for a deep-chested dog.

Do beagles need booties in winter?

Many beagles never wear them. Booties earn their place when your routes are salted or gritted, when ice packs between the toes, or when your beagle keeps stopping to lift its paws on cold pavement.

The beagle standard asks for feet that are "close, round and firm" with pads "full and hard", Those are working feet. Still, city winters are not hunting fields. Toronto Humane Society, in its advice for dogs that dislike snow boots, suggests paw balm, checking paws for ice, snow or salt build-up after walks, and shorter walks as fallback options for dogs that refuse boots.

How to measure paw width

Ruffwear's method works for any brand that sizes by width: put a sheet of paper on the floor, have your beagle stand on it, lift the opposite paw so the weight goes onto the one you are measuring, mark both sides of the widest part with a pen held straight up and down, then measure between the marks. Do it for a rear paw too. Ruffwear notes that rear paws are often smaller, and for boots it says, "If you're in between sizes, we recommend sizing down." Its boot chart runs from 1.50 to 3.25 inches in quarter-inch steps.

Fit: not too tight, not too loose

Toronto Humane Society warns that boots that are too tight "may restrict circulation or pinch, while loose boots can slip off or make walking awkward," and that a poor fit may be why a dog resists wearing them in the first place. A good boot sits snug at the strap with the toes able to spread inside.

Which booties do owners say actually stay on?

The booties that stay on have a strap that closes above the wrist or ankle, a sole sized to the paw width, and a dog that has practiced wearing them indoors. Fit and training matter more than the price.

In the same hound forum thread, one owner wrote that "most booties fall off" their dogs' feet and asked what stays on. The answers are a good summary of the options. One person suggested boots with a hook-and-loop closure around the "ankle" part. Another reported that their dog's Muttluks "do stay on her feet," but that the first outings each year bring a lot of high prancing before the dog settles. Several owners described that comical high step, and Toronto Humane Society calls it normal at first.

How to get your beagle used to a coat and booties: step by step

Beagles accept gear fastest when it predicts food and the walk. If your dog already makes a game of dodging the harness, read our guide on the beagle who runs from the leash first, because the same chase can start with a coat. Then work through this list over several short sessions:

  1. Leave the gear on the floor. Put the coat and boots near the food bowl or bed and scatter a few treats around them. Toronto Humane Society suggests letting the dog sniff and get familiar with boots "before even trying to put them on."
  2. Reward contact. Treat for touching the coat, then for letting you lay it on the back, then for each strap that closes. Fasten nothing that makes your beagle freeze or back away.
  3. Handle the feet. Hold each paw for a second, treat, release. Then touch the paw with a boot, treat, release.
  4. One boot at a time. Put a single front boot on for a few seconds, treat, take it off. Build to two, then four.
  5. Walk indoors. Let your beagle wear the full set around the house for short spells, ideally while you hide a few treats for it to sniff out. Our scent games for beagles make perfect distractions here.
  6. Check the fit after moving. After five minutes of walking around, look for straps that slipped, boots that twisted and rubbing at the armpits.
  7. First outdoor walks short. Go out for a few minutes on an easy surface and expect high stepping. Keep the boots on every time you go out in the snow, even for short walks, so they become part of the routine.

Common mistakes with beagle winter gear

  • Buying by weight or "medium dog" labels. Weight tells you nothing about girth. Measure the chest.
  • Sizing a coat by back length only. On a deep-chested beagle it can be too tight behind the front legs.
  • No belly strap. A coat that only buckles at the neck will swing to the side within a block of sniffing.
  • Covering the harness clip. If you can not clip the leash through the coat, you end up with the leash on the collar, which is a poor swap for a beagle that pulls toward every scent.
  • Same boot size for front and rear. Measure both; rear paws are often smaller.
  • Strap below the joint. A boot strap that sits on the narrowest part of the leg slides straight down and off.
  • First try on the snowy sidewalk. Boots introduced outdoors, cold and in a hurry, usually end up in a drawer. Practice indoors.
  • Leaving gear on unsupervised. A restless young beagle (see when beagles calm down) will chew straps, and loose gear can catch on crates and furniture.

Frequently asked questions

What size coat does a beagle wear?

There is no single beagle size: the breed standard has two height varieties, 13 inches and under and over 13 up to 15 inches, and chest depth varies a lot between dogs. Measure the chest just behind the front legs and compare it with the specific brand's chart.

Should a beagle wear a coat in the snow?

A fit adult beagle with a dense coat often does fine on a short, active walk. Puppies, seniors, lean or thin-coated beagles, and dogs out on slow, wet walks benefit most. Watch for shivering, paw lifting and a tucked tail.

Why does my beagle's coat keep sliding to one side?

Usually because it has no belly strap, or the strap is too loose or sits too far back. A wrap coat or a shell with a wide adjustable strap behind the front legs anchors it against all that sniffing.

Should I size beagle boots up or down?

Follow the brand's rule. Ruffwear, for example, says to size down for boots if your dog is between sizes, and to measure the rear paws separately because they are often smaller.

My beagle walks like a robot in boots. Will that stop?

For most dogs it does. The high step is common at first. Keep early sessions short and indoors, reward normal walking, and make the boots part of every snowy walk so they become routine.

Can a beagle wear a coat and a harness together?

Yes. Choose a coat with a leash opening, or put a thin coat under the harness. Check that the harness still fits, because a layer under it adds girth.

The bottom line

Beagle winter coats and booties are not a must for every hound, but for puppies, seniors, lean or thin-coated beagles and anyone walking salted streets, the right gear makes winter walks longer and happier. Measure the chest first, pick a coat with a belly strap and a leash opening, size boots by paw width, and spend a few short sessions indoors before the first snowy walk.
