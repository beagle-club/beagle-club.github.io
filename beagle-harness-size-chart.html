Beagle Harness Size Chart: Which Size Fits a Beagle in Ruffwear, Julius-K9, Kong, 2 Hounds and Other Popular Brands

One chest measurement, five brand charts, and what other beagle owners learned the hard way about Small, Medium and "Size 0".

Home›Blog›Beagle Harness Size Chart

Published: 2026-10-15 · Updated: 2026-10-15 · Beagle Club Editors

Ask a room of owners which beagle harness size chart they trusted and you will hear the same story: the label said "beagle", the harness arrived, and it either gaped at the chest or pinched behind the elbows. That is because Ruffwear, Julius-K9, KONG, 2 Hounds Design and PetSafe all size by chest girth, but each one draws its size lines in different places, and a few of them even disagree about which size a typical beagle belongs in. Below you get every chart side by side, a size finder that checks your dog's measurement against all of them at once, and the fit checks experienced beagle owners rely on.

Quick answer

  • Measure first, then pick the brand size. A beagle's chest girth, not its weight or breed, decides the size.
  • Brands that name beagles in their own charts put them in Small (2 Hounds Freedom, 20–24 in girth), Size 0 (Julius-K9 IDC, 58–76 cm) and Large (KONG Reflective Pathfinder, 61–84 cm).
  • Ruffwear Front Range Small covers 22–27 in, so most beagles in that girth band wear Small; a slim 13-inch beagle may sit in XS (17–22 in).
  • PetSafe Easy Walk Small/Medium covers 19–26 in girth, the band many adult beagles fall into.
  • Between two sizes? Ruffwear and PetSafe say go larger; 2 Hounds says choose the smallest size that still fits.

What size harness does a beagle wear?

Most adult beagles wear a Small in Ruffwear and 2 Hounds Design, a Size 0 in the Julius-K9 IDC Powerharness, and a Small/Medium in the PetSafe Easy Walk, but the only reliable answer comes from a tape measure. Beagles vary a lot in size, so two dogs of the same breed can need different sizes in the same brand.

The breed is officially split into two height varieties in the US: the American Kennel Club recognizes a 13-inch variety for hounds under 13 inches and a 15-inch variety for hounds between 13 and 15 inches, while the UK Kennel Club allows up to 16 inches. Across the breed, beagles generally weigh between 18 and 35 lb, according to the Wikipedia entry on the Beagle. A compact 13-inch female and a deep-chested 15-inch male can easily be two harness sizes apart.

Why weight alone misleads

Weight bands on harness charts are approximate, and several brands say so outright. PetSafe's own chart says measuring is the most accurate way to choose and that its weight ranges are only approximate. A 30 lb beagle carrying extra weight around the ribs has a bigger girth than a lean, muscular 30 lb hunting beagle, and if your dog has gained weight recently, the harness that fit last spring may no longer close. Ruffwear Front Range

Ruffwear measures "around the widest part of the rib cage" and tells owners who fall between sizes to choose the larger one. Its Front Range fit guide lists XS at 17–22 in (43–56 cm) and Small at 22–27 in (56–69 cm). The ranges touch rather than overlap, so a beagle measuring exactly 22 in is the classic "between sizes" dog, and Ruffwear's own rule sends it to Small. Owners of slimmer 13-inch beagles under about 22 in will usually end up in XS.

2 Hounds Design Freedom No-Pull

2 Hounds is the only brand here whose rule runs the other way. Its Freedom harness size chart asks you to measure "the largest part of the chest, right behind the front legs" and then choose the smallest size that still fits, because the front straps sit better. Their own example: a dog measuring exactly 28 in still goes in Medium. The chart puts Beagle in Small (20–24 in, 25–35 lbs). Medium covers 24–28 in and comes in a 5/8 in width, which the brand recommends for dogs under 40 lbs, so a big beagle that outgrows Small can still wear the narrower Medium.

Julius-K9 IDC Powerharness

Julius-K9 sizes are numbered rather than lettered, and the Powerharness lists Beagle under Size 0 (58–76 cm chest girth, 14–25 kg). The Julius-K9 size guide published by UK stockist Millbry Hill also sets Mini at 49–67 cm for 7–15 kg dogs, which covers small or slim beagles. The measuring spot is different from the other brands: for sizes 0 to 4 you place four fingers behind the front legs and measure around the chest at the finger furthest from the legs. That is further back than the "right behind the front legs" spot 2 Hounds uses, so a measurement taken for one brand is not automatically valid for the other.

KONG Reflective Pathfinder

KONG-branded harnesses are sold through retailers, and the published sizing depends on the model. For the Reflective Pathfinder, Petbarn's listing gives Medium as 51–74 cm chest girth (neck up to 51 cm, dogs up to 23 kg) and Large as 61–84 cm (neck up to 66 cm, up to 41 kg), and it names Beagle as an example breed for Large. Because the Medium range also covers most beagle girths, it is easy to land in the squeeze one beagle owner describes further down: one size too snug, the next one too loose. If your beagle measures 61–74 cm, both sizes technically fit, and the neck opening is often the deciding factor.

Other popular brands: PetSafe Easy Walk

The front-clip PetSafe Easy Walk is sized by chest width as well as girth, which helps with the beagle's broad-chest, narrow-waist build. The PetSafe Easy Walk size chart lists Small/Medium at 19–26 in girth and 11–13 in chest width (about 20–30 lbs) and Medium at 22–32 in girth and 12–15 in chest width (about 25–50 lbs). PetSafe advises choosing the larger size when your dog falls between two.

Why do brands put beagles in different sizes?

Because each brand draws its size bands differently, measures at a slightly different spot on the chest, and builds its breed examples around an "average" dog that may not match yours. The labels Small, Medium and Size 0 are not standard units.

Look at the table again and the conflicts jump out. 2 Hounds puts beagles in Small, KONG names them in Large, and Julius-K9 picks a numbered middle size. None of those is wrong. Each is a guess about a typical dog, and beagles are not a uniform breed: the 13-inch and 15-inch varieties, show lines versus field lines, and young versus middle-aged dogs all change the shape of the chest.

Overlapping ranges are a feature, not a mistake

Julius-K9 and KONG ranges overlap heavily (Julius Mini ends at 67 cm, Size 0 starts at 58 cm). Overlap gives you a choice, but it also means a dog at the top of one size and the bottom of the next can wear either. A practical way to decide is to pick the size where your dog's girth sits closest to the middle of the range, so the straps have room to adjust in both directions as weight and coat change.

Deep chest, narrow waist

Beagles have a broad chest that narrows to a tapered waist. A harness with a single girth strap set far back can slide toward the waist, while the front can sit loose on a beagle with narrow shoulders. That is how a harness can match the girth on paper and still slip around, as one owner below describes.

How do you measure a beagle for a harness?

Have your beagle standing, wrap a soft fabric tape around the widest part of the rib cage just behind the front legs, keep it snug without squeezing, and read the number in both inches and centimeters. Then check where your chosen brand wants the tape, because Julius-K9 measures a little further back.

This short video from Pet Circle, presented by their veterinarian Dr. Carla, opens with how to measure a dog for a harness and then shows how vest, step-in, padded chest, dual-strap and front-clip harnesses should sit once fitted. It is worth watching before you adjust any of the straps.

Measuring tips that save a return

  • Measure standing, not sitting. A sitting beagle bunches up through the chest and gives a smaller reading.
  • Use a cloth tape. No tape? Wrap a piece of string or a phone charger cable, mark it, and lay it flat against a ruler.
  • Measure twice, on two days. A beagle that has just eaten or is wriggling toward a treat will give you a different number.
  • Note the neck too. KONG publishes a maximum neck size per harness size, and a big-necked beagle can be the reason a Medium will not go over the head.
  • Write down both units. Julius-K9 and KONG publish in centimeters, Ruffwear, 2 Hounds and PetSafe in inches; 1 inch is 2.54 cm.

What other beagle owners report about harness sizes

Owners who share their experience most often say the girth fits on paper but the harness slips, gapes at the shoulders, or lets a determined beagle back out, which is why adjustability matters as much as the size label.

A 2026 r/beagle thread titled "Any harness recommendations? I've tried 3 and they never fit" sums up the problem. The owner had tried two KONG mediums and a large: the mediums were too small, and the large worked only with the front chest straps fully tightened, so it still slipped around while the dog sniffed. The replies read like a field test of the brands in this chart:

  • One owner uses a Julius-K9 Powerharness in Size 0 on a heavier beagle, with the chest strap at its smallest setting and the belly strap let out as far as it goes, and says it is the first harness the dog tolerates being put on.
  • Several owners recommended Ruffwear; one with a narrow-shouldered beagle uses the three-buckle Webmaster because the two rear straps keep it secure even when the front sits a bit loose.
  • One owner uses the 2 Hounds Freedom No-Pull for its many adjustment points and, for a flight-risk beagle, clips a leash splitter to a martingale collar as backup.
  • Another owner with a 38 lb beagle settled on a Large in one harness, after a Medium in another model sat too deep at the front strap and could not be adjusted.

The pattern is consistent with the charts: beagles sit right on the boundary between two sizes in most brands, so the harness with more adjustment points usually wins. Step-by-step: fitting the harness once it arrives

Use this checklist before the first walk. It takes five minutes and catches most sizing problems before they turn into rubbed armpits or an escaped hound.

  1. Loosen every strap first, then put the harness on and tighten gradually, side to side, so the chest plate stays centered.
  2. Check the two-finger gap. You should be able to slide two flat fingers under each strap, but not your whole hand.
  3. Check elbow clearance. Watch your beagle walk: the girth strap or buckle should not touch the back of the front legs or the armpit. Julius-K9's measuring guide makes the same point about full front-leg swing.
  4. Do the back-out test indoors. Hold the leash and gently step backward so the harness is pulled toward the head. If the neck loop slides over the ears, tighten it or try the smaller size.
  5. Check the front-clip position on no-pull models like the Freedom or Easy Walk: the clip should sit on the breastbone, not swing down between the legs.
  6. Walk ten minutes, then re-check. Straps settle and webbing stretches slightly; re-tighten after the first walk and again after a week.
  7. Look for rub marks behind the elbows and under the chest after the first few longer walks.

A well-fitted harness also makes the daily routine easier on everyone. A good, sniff-heavy walk before you leave the house is one of the simplest ways to help with the issues covered in how long beagles can be left alone.

Common beagle harness sizing mistakes

The most common mistake is ordering by breed or weight instead of girth; the second is ignoring each brand's own "between sizes" rule.

Mistakes we see again and again

  • Buying the size the listing names for beagles. As the table shows, brands disagree: Small, Size 0 and Large are all "the beagle size" somewhere.
  • Reusing one measurement for every brand. Julius-K9 measures four fingers behind the front legs; 2 Hounds measures right behind them. The numbers can differ.
  • Applying the wrong tie rule. At exactly 24 in, 2 Hounds says Small (smallest that fits), while at exactly 22 in Ruffwear says Small (go larger). Following the wrong rule is how owners end up with a harness at the very end of its adjustment.
  • Sizing a puppy for the adult dog. A harness at its tightest setting on a growing puppy is easy to slip out of. Size for today's girth and plan to replace it.
  • Forgetting the neck. A harness that fits the chest but not over the head is still the wrong size; check the neck limit where brands publish one.
  • Tightening until it cannot move. Too tight restricts the shoulders and rubs; too loose lets a beagle that has caught a scent reverse straight out of it.

When should you re-measure your beagle?

Re-measure every few weeks while your beagle is growing, after any noticeable weight gain or loss, and whenever the harness starts to slip, gape or rub. A few changes in girth can move a beagle across a size boundary.

Puppies

Beagle puppies grow out of harnesses quickly. Choose a model with a wide adjustment range, buy for the current girth, and check the fit weekly. Expect to buy at least one more size before your puppy is fully grown.

Adults and seniors

Adult beagles mostly change size with weight. If you are working on a diet plan with your vet, re-measure each time the dog drops a noticeable amount, because the straps that were snug at the heavier weight may let a slimmer beagle wriggle free. Seniors with stiff shoulders often do better in a step-in or buckle-sided design that does not need to go over the head.

Frequently asked questions

What size harness fits a beagle in Ruffwear?

Most adult beagles with a 22–27 in chest girth wear a Ruffwear Small in the Front Range. Slim beagles under 22 in usually fit XS (17–22 in). Ruffwear says to choose the larger size if your dog is between sizes.

Is a beagle a Size 0 or Mini in Julius-K9?

Julius-K9 lists Beagle under Size 0, with a 58–76 cm chest girth for dogs of 14–25 kg. Smaller or slim beagles can fit the Mini (49–67 cm, 7–15 kg). Because the ranges overlap, measure four fingers behind the front legs and pick the size where your dog sits nearer the middle.

What size 2 Hounds Freedom harness does a beagle need?

2 Hounds Design names Beagle in its Small size (20–24 in chest, 25–35 lbs). If your beagle measures more than 24 in, the Medium (24–28 in) is next, and the 5/8 in width is recommended for dogs under 40 lbs. The brand says to pick the smallest size that still fits.

Why does my beagle need Medium in one brand and Small in another?

Each brand draws its size bands differently and measures at a slightly different point on the chest. A 24 in beagle is Small in Ruffwear and Small in 2 Hounds, but inside both Small/Medium and Medium in PetSafe. Always compare your dog's girth with the specific brand's chart.

Should I size up or down if my beagle is between sizes?

Follow the brand's own rule. Ruffwear and PetSafe recommend the larger size; 2 Hounds Design recommends the smallest size that still fits. For overlapping ranges such as Julius-K9 and KONG, choose the size where the girth sits closest to the middle.

How tight should a harness be on a beagle?

Snug enough that you can slide two flat fingers under the straps but not your whole hand, with no contact between the girth strap and the back of the front legs. Do a gentle back-out test indoors before trusting it outside.

The bottom line on beagle harness sizes

A beagle harness size chart is only useful once you know your own dog's chest girth. Measure standing, note inches and centimeters, then read each brand's chart with its own between-sizes rule: Small or XS in Ruffwear, Small in 2 Hounds, Size 0 or Mini in Julius-K9, Medium or Large in KONG, and Small/Medium or Medium in PetSafe cover most beagles. When your dog lands on a boundary, the harness with more adjustment points is usually the one beagle owners end up keeping.
